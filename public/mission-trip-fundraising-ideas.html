Mission Trip Fundraising Ideas: 30 Ways to Fund Your Trip

Thirty practical ways for church teams to raise mission trip support — from the support letter that does most of the work to events, product sales, and online campaigns that engage your whole community. This guide is about how to raise the money; for what a trip actually costs, see the budget guide.

Fundraising is the step that derails more mission trips than any other — not because the money is not there, but because teams scatter their effort across gimmicks instead of working the few methods that actually move dollars. The ideas below are organized from highest-yield to nice-to-have. Start at the top.

One principle runs through all of them: people give to people and to a clear mission, not to a generic cause. Every idea here works better when each team member has a personal story, a specific goal, and a simple way for someone to give in one tap. If you want the wider planning context, this is Step 5 of the full guide to planning a mission trip.

Personal Support-Raising (Start Here)

This is the engine. Done well, personal support-raising funds the majority of most trips before a single event happens.

  1. The support letter. Each team member writes 50-150 people — family, friends, coworkers, neighbors, former small-group members. Tell your story, explain the trip, name the goal, and make the ask plainly.
  2. The personal text + donation link. The letter gets the context; a short personal text with a one-tap giving link gets the gift. Send it to your warmest contacts first.
  3. Face-to-face asks. The highest-converting method of all. Ask your ten closest supporters in person or on a call. Most people are bad at this until someone coaches them — so coach them.
  4. The follow-up. Most gifts come after the second contact, not the first. Build a one-week follow-up into every ask. "Did you get a chance to think about it?" is not pushy; it is normal.
  5. Thank-you + update loop. Donors who feel thanked and informed give again next trip. Send a thank-you within 48 hours and a photo update from the field.

Church-Based Fundraising

Your sending church is your largest single source of support and credibility. Work with leadership early.

  1. A dedicated missions offering. Ask for one Sunday where the offering (or a designated portion) goes to the team.
  2. Commissioning Sunday. Have the team share testimonies before they go. Vision plus a giving moment is powerful.
  3. Matching gift from the missions budget. Many churches will match team-raised funds up to a cap. Ask.
  4. Sponsor-a-team-member board. A physical or digital board where the congregation claims a share of a member's goal.
  5. Small-group and Sunday-school challenges. Invite classes to adopt a team member or fund a specific line item (a week of meals, a project's materials).

Events That Engage the Whole Church

Events rarely out-earn support letters per hour invested, but they build momentum and pull in people outside the team's networks.

  1. Fundraiser dinner or banquet. Sell tickets, share the vision, take a free-will offering at the end.
  2. Pancake or spaghetti breakfast. Low-cost, high-turnout, easy for volunteers.
  3. Service auction. Team members auction skills — babysitting, lawn care, music lessons, home repair.
  4. Silent auction of donated goods. Local businesses often donate in exchange for recognition.
  5. Car wash or yard-cleanup day. Classic for youth teams; pairs well with a donation jar rather than a fixed price.
  6. Talent night or concert. Charge admission and sell concessions.
  7. 5K or fun run. Charge registration; let runners collect per-mile pledges.

Product & Service Sales

Selling something gives reluctant givers an easy "yes." Keep your margins honest — a $20 item that nets $4 is a lot of work for the return.

  1. Custom trip t-shirts or hoodies. Supporters wear them and become walking advertisements.
  2. Coffee or chocolate sales. Especially meaningful if sourced from your destination country.
  3. Bake sales and cookie-by-the-dozen pre-orders. Pre-orders beat day-of sales every time.
  4. Restaurant spirit nights. Many chains donate 10-20% of sales on a chosen night.
  5. Seasonal sales. Wreaths and poinsettias in December, flowers for Mother's Day, mums in fall.
  6. Discount/coupon cards. Partner with local businesses for a card of deals you sell.

Online & Digital Campaigns

Digital tools multiply every other method on this list — they make giving instant and sharing effortless.

  1. Individual online donation pages. A personal page with photo, story, and live goal thermometer converts far better than "mail a check."
  2. Social media giving days. Pick a date, build anticipation for a week, and push hard for 24 hours.
  3. Short video appeals. A 60-second phone video of a team member sharing why they are going outperforms any flyer.
  4. Email + text follow-ups. Segment who has given and who has not; only chase the latter.
  5. Employer matching gifts. Many companies match charitable donations — remind supporters to check.
  6. QR codes everywhere. On the bulletin, the t-shirts, the dinner table tents — point them all at the donation page.
  7. Recurring monthly support. For longer trips, $25/month from 20 people funds a serious chunk painlessly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most effective mission trip fundraising method?

Personal support letters are consistently the highest-yielding method. A team member who writes 50-150 people and follows up personally typically raises more than any single event. Events and product sales work best as supplements that engage the wider church, not as the primary strategy.

How do you raise money for a mission trip fast?

The fastest path is a focused support-letter push paired with an online donation page you can share by text and social media. Send personalized asks to your warmest contacts first, make giving a single tap, and follow up within a week.

Should each team member fundraise individually or as a team?

Use both. Give each person an individual goal and donation page, and run team-wide efforts that get split across members who are behind. The combination keeps motivated givers engaged and supports members with smaller networks.

Is it okay to ask people directly to support a mission trip?

Yes. You are inviting people to participate in ministry, not begging. Frame the ask around the work being done and the chance to be part of it — many who give later say they were glad to be invited.

How do online mission trip donation pages work?

Each team member gets a personal page with their photo, story, and goal. Supporters give by card or bank transfer, the gift is credited toward that person's goal, and team-wide gifts are split automatically. Purpose-built platforms handle the split penny-accurately and issue receipts.
