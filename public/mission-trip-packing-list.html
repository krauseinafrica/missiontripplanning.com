Mission Trip Packing List: What to Bring

A complete, printable packing list for short-term mission teams — organized by category so nothing essential gets left behind. This is about what to physically pack; for the time-based planning tasks leading up to departure, use the planning checklist.

Packing for a mission trip is different from packing for a vacation. You are traveling to serve, often in a modest cultural context, sometimes carrying shared ministry materials, and usually with strict luggage limits. The goal is to bring everything you genuinely need, nothing you do not, and to keep the irreplaceable items on your body or in your carry-on.

Print this list and check off each item as it goes in the bag. Share it with your whole team so everyone packs consistently — and so the person assigned the first-aid kit or the children's-ministry supplies actually brings them. Always confirm destination-specific needs with your trip leader and local partner.

1. Essential Documents (Carry-On Only)

If you lose these, the trip stops. Keep originals on you and copies in a separate bag.

  • Passport (valid at least 6 months beyond your return date) + two photocopies
  • Visa or entry documents, if required for your destination
  • Printed flight itinerary and boarding passes
  • Travel insurance card and policy number
  • Signed liability waiver and medical release (digital copy too)
  • Emergency contact card and your local partner's address/phone
  • A small amount of local currency plus one backup card
  • Passport-sized photos (some countries require them on arrival)

2. Clothing (Modest & Climate-Appropriate)

Ask your local partner about cultural norms before you pack — modesty expectations vary widely. Pack layers and quick-dry fabrics; plan to do laundry rather than bring a fresh outfit per day.

  • Work clothes you can get dirty (for construction or outdoor projects)
  • Modest everyday outfits appropriate to the culture
  • One set of "nicer" clothes for church services or meeting partners
  • Comfortable, broken-in closed-toe shoes + sandals
  • Rain jacket or poncho; light layers for temperature swings
  • Hat, sunglasses, and a swimsuit only if appropriate/modest
  • Enough underwear and socks; one outfit in your carry-on

3. Toiletries & Personal Health Kit

  • Prescription medication (carry-on, original labels, extra days' supply)
  • Basic first aid: bandages, antiseptic, pain reliever, anti-diarrheal, antihistamine
  • Insect repellent (DEET) and high-SPF sunscreen
  • Hand sanitizer and travel pack of wet wipes
  • Toothbrush, toothpaste, deodorant, soap, shampoo (travel sizes)
  • Any required vaccination records (e.g., yellow fever certificate)
  • Water purification tablets or filter bottle if advised
  • Feminine hygiene products (may be hard to find locally)

4. Ministry & Project Materials

Coordinate these as a team so items are not duplicated and the weight is shared across everyone's checked allowance.

  • Bible and a personal journal or devotional
  • VBS / children's ministry supplies (crafts, stickers, coloring sheets)
  • Sports equipment or instruments for outreach, if planned
  • Project-specific tools or materials assigned by your leader
  • Small gifts for host families or local partners
  • Printed testimony or translated gospel materials, if used

5. Electronics & Practical Extras

  • Phone + charger; universal power adapter for your destination
  • Portable battery pack (carry-on only — never checked)
  • Headlamp or flashlight for areas with unreliable power
  • Reusable water bottle and a few snacks for travel days
  • Ziploc bags, a luggage lock, and a packable daypack
  • Earplugs, eye mask, and a neck pillow for long flights
  • A small notebook and pens that work without internet

What NOT to Bring

  • Expensive jewelry, watches, or valuables you would hate to lose
  • Flashy or immodest clothing that clashes with local culture
  • More electronics than you need — they make you a target and add weight
  • Anything illegal or restricted at your destination (check medications and drones)
  • A full bag with no room — leave space for shared supplies and gifts you carry home

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I pack for a mission trip?

Pack in five priority tiers: essential documents, modest climate-appropriate clothing, a personal toiletry and health kit, ministry materials for your project, and a carry-on with everything you cannot afford to lose. Keep your passport, medication, and one change of clothes in your carry-on, never in checked luggage.

What should you NOT bring on a mission trip?

Leave behind expensive jewelry, valuables, immodest or flashy clothing, excessive electronics, and anything illegal or restricted at your destination. Avoid over-packing — leave room for ministry supplies and gifts.

How much luggage should I bring?

Most teams limit each person to one checked bag plus a carry-on, often reserving part of the checked allowance for shared supplies. Confirm airline weight limits early, weigh your bag at home, and leave margin for distributed items.

Should I pack medication in checked or carry-on luggage?

Always carry-on, in the original labeled container, with enough for the full trip plus a few extra days and a copy of the prescription. Checked bags get delayed or lost; your medication should never be among them.

Is there a printable mission trip packing list?

Yes — this page is organized by category so you can print it and check off items as you pack. Share it with your whole team so everyone packs consistently and no one forgets shared ministry materials.
